# Evidence Guidelines

## Use only authorized material

Use material you created, received with permission, or are otherwise entitled to process. Do not scrape, download, identify, or republish another person's private account, messages, health information, contact details, or images without clear authorization.

## Rank sources

| Evidence | Recommended use | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clearly attributed therapy transcript | Observable response sequence and wording patterns | High |
| User correction tied to a specific interaction | Calibration of a local mirror | High |
| Public professional writing | Public frameworks and stated approach | Medium |
| Private social writing | Avoid by default; never infer attitudes toward the user | Low |
| AI summary or noisy ASR | Candidate only; verify against primary material | Low |

## Extract patterns, not identity

Record only reusable observations such as:

- how the person sets an agenda;
- how they ask for context or sequence;
- whether they reflect before challenging;
- how they handle uncertainty, boundaries, and repair;
- what kinds of metaphors or structured exercises they use.

Avoid biographical facts, names, locations, credentials, health details, client details, relationship details, contact data, and direct quotation unless essential and authorized.

## Treat ambiguity honestly

Keep an uncertainty note when speaker labels are missing, transcription is unreliable, a source conflicts with another source, or a behavior appears in only one context. Do not turn a single incident into a personality rule.
